# THE DEPARTMENT OF CONTINUITY

**Internal designation:** Office of Records Integrity — Continuity Adjustment Unit  
**Format:** late-1990s institutional training film  
**Target runtime:** 2:36  
**Delivery:** 3840×2160, 24fps, 1.66:1 image inside 16:9  
**Status:** screenplay and pre-production lock; no paid generation yet

## Logline

A continuity technician follows approved procedure to resolve a coffee mug that
exists before its purchase. Each correction transfers the contradiction into a
person, a memory and finally the training film itself—until the Department
chooses the version of reality with the strongest paperwork.

## Governing idea

> Bureaucracy does not preserve reality. It decides which reality receives
> paperwork.

The three apparent “cases” are one causal chain:

1. A premature mug forces its purchase date backward.
2. The new purchase date conflicts with a hospital admission, displacing a
   patient from the record.
3. The displaced patient recurs wherever reality has spare background space.
4. His undocumented childhood is allocated to another employee.
5. The technician objects, creating a personnel discrepancy.
6. The instructional film is retained as the authoritative account; the
   technician is updated to agree with it.

## Why this is not SomniTech again

SomniTech was an anthology of nightmare procedures that broke down. This film
has one accumulating consequence and one protagonist who becomes responsible
for it. The image never “glitches.” In fact, the film becomes cleaner and more
authoritative as lived reality deteriorates.

## Non-negotiable creative rules

- No VHS-glitch climax, melting faces or random continuity mistakes.
- No “the viewer is the anomaly” reveal.
- No demonic narrator, red warning text or horror sting.
- Every retained discrepancy is listed in the continuity ledger.
- All plot-critical text, dates, forms and photographs are composited in post.
- One impossible mechanism per shot.
- The narrator never marvels and never uses supernatural vocabulary.
- A failed generation is not an aesthetic. Accidental errors are rejected.
- After two failed final-quality generations, redesign the shot.

## Production gates

1. Approve screenplay.
2. Approve character and location packs.
3. Build a silent animatic from approved keyframes.
4. Record final narration and lock the edit.
5. Produce a 25-second proof: mug correction through hospital displacement.
6. Review proof at full resolution with sound.
7. Only then generate the remaining final-quality motion.
