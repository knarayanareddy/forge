# THE DEPARTMENT OF CONTINUITY

**Office of Records Integrity — Continuity Adjustment Unit**  
Shooting screenplay · Draft 1 · Target runtime 2:36

## Cast

- **MARA VOSS, 38** — continuity technician. Precise, observant, neither
  rebellious nor naïve. Gray field jacket, oxblood notebook, red binder.
- **JUNE HAVEL, 50s** — payroll clerk. Dry, practical.
- **PETER GRAY, 40s** — hospital patient displaced from the record. Gray suit,
  green umbrella, folded newspaper.
- **LINA ORTIZ, 30s** — records employee who receives Peter’s childhood.
- **MRS. GRAY, 70s** — Peter’s mother.
- **NARRATOR** — mature institutional voice. Never visible.

The narrator is not sinister. It is the sound of a policy being read correctly.

---

## ACT I — AUTHORITY

### 1. LEADER / TITLE — 0:00–0:06

Black leader. Projector chatter, then a restrained three-note educational-film
chime.

A cream optical title, perfectly typeset:

```text
OFFICE OF RECORDS INTEGRITY
CONTINUITY ADJUSTMENT UNIT

MODULE 4 — SECONDARY DISPLACEMENT
```

A red pencil enters frame and checks `AUTHORIZED FOR INTERNAL OCCURRENCE`.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**  
Welcome to the Continuity Adjustment Unit.

### 2. TRAINING ROOM — 0:06–0:24

A squared, static wide of a windowless training room. Nicotine-cream walls.
Celadon lower paint. A steel lectern. Three drawers behind it:

```text
PROPERTY
EVENT
PERSON
```

The room is empty. On the lectern sits a red binder.

Cut to three matched institutional inserts. One red Case 4-19 slip routes through
steel trays marked `PROPERTY`, then `EVENT`, then `PERSON`. The last tray’s bell
sounds before the paper arrives.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**  
A contradiction is not two events. It is two records competing for one event.
Determine which account can be maintained.

Mara enters, picks up the binder and opens Case 4-19.

---

## ACT II — TRANSFER

### 3. CASE 4-19: PREMATURE PROPERTY — 0:24–0:58

#### Payroll office

A locked 40mm master. June reaches toward an empty patch of desk.

Cut tighter. A cream ceramic mug with one cobalt stripe is now beneath her hand.
The change happens between shots, not as a morph. Steam rises. Its handle is
chipped. A faint lipstick mark touches the rim.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**  
At eight forty-three Monday, June Havel reported a mug three days before
purchase.

Mara places a glass evidence dome over the mug without touching it.

**MARA**  
It’s warm.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**  
Temperature is not proof. Premature property should not be used. Use creates
history.

#### Record insert

An untouched cafeteria receipt reads:

```text
PURCHASER: JUNE HAVEL
LOCATION: CAFETERIA
PURCHASE DATE: THURSDAY 14
TIME: 08:43
```

The mug’s evidence tag reads:

```text
OBSERVED: MONDAY 11
```

Mara stamps the receipt:

```text
AUTHORITATIVE
```

Under the stamp impact, the purchase date changes:

```text
PURCHASE DATE: FRIDAY 8
```

No other part of the paper moves.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**  
The future receipt was designated authoritative. The purchase moved to Friday.

#### Hospital record

A clinical photograph: June admitted to St. Orison Hospital on Friday the
eighth.

Mara slides the revised receipt across the lower half of the photograph. While
the patient is fully occluded, the state switches. As the receipt clears, the
hospital name card reads `PETER GRAY`, and Peter occupies the bed.

The pillow indentation, shadows, glare and room remain identical.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**  
June’s cafeteria purchase conflicted with her hospital admission. The admission
was retained by assigning it to Peter Gray.

**MARA**  
To whom?

The narrator does not answer immediately.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**  
Secondary displacement is normal.

---

### 4. CASE 4-19A: UNASSIGNED EVENT — 0:58–1:34

#### Street surveillance

A locked wide outside a government cafeteria. A clock is visible. A couple talk
in the foreground. Peter crosses the deep background right-to-left carrying a
green umbrella and folded newspaper.

At exactly eleven seconds, the same bicycle bell, heel rhythm, newspaper snap
and cough repeat. Peter crosses again on the identical path.

The second pass is frame-accurate until its final half-second. Peter stops. He
turns toward the surveillance camera.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**  
Peter’s Friday location was now St. Orison. His street record became an
unassigned event.

On the monitor, Peter looks directly toward Mara.

**PETER (ON MONITOR)**  
Where should I have been?

Mara reaches toward the intercom.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**  
Do not answer. An answer is a location.

Mara types into the continuity log:

```text
STATUS: RECURRING PEDESTRIAN
FREQUENCY: ORDINARY
```

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**  
Mara documented Peter as recurring. Witness recollection reconciled immediately.

The cafeteria couple stop talking. Without looking toward Peter:

**WOMAN**  
He walks here every day.

The clock resumes. Peter crosses a third time. This pass is accepted; nobody
reacts.

On the couple’s table sits June’s cobalt-striped mug.

Peter’s status panel updates:

```text
PERSONNEL LINK: RELEASED
COMPATIBLE EMPLOYEE FILE: LINA ORTIZ
```

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**  
Linked cases do not imply causation. Released histories go to the first
compatible employee file.

---

### 5. CASE 4-19B: PERSONNEL ALLOCATION — 1:34–2:17

#### Records office

Lina enters frame-left after lunch carrying a scuffed red child’s satchel and a
small wooden airplane. She treats both as ordinary.

Peter waits beside Mara. He recognizes the airplane before he recognizes Lina.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**  
Lina Ortiz returned from lunch carrying Peter’s documented childhood.

Insert: Peter’s family photograph before correction. Child Peter stands beside
Mrs. Gray.

Under one fluorescent flicker, the locked photograph changes. Child Lina now
stands beside Mrs. Gray. Every adult, shadow, crease and frame reflection is
pixel-identical.

Lina now has the small scar formerly above Peter’s left eyebrow.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**  
Lina retained photographed birthdays. Peter retained nightmares and undocumented
affection.

**MARA**  
They remember the same mother.

#### Recognition test

Mrs. Gray stands in a kitchen doorway. Peter is frame-left; Lina frame-right.
Mrs. Gray looks to Peter with immediate recognition.

A clerk passes the approved allocation form across lens, filling the frame.
When it clears—same camera, same blocking—Mrs. Gray is looking at Lina.

Peter has not moved. His expression barely changes.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**  
Recognition follows the approved record.

**MARA**  
They cannot both be true.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**  
Truth is not a continuity requirement.

Mara closes the binder.

Only now does the photograph update again. In its back row stands adult Mara,
unchanged, holding the red binder. The cobalt-striped mug rests on a child’s
desk.

This is the consequence of her objection, not unexplained foreshadowing. No
character comments on either clue.

---

## ACT III — RECONCILIATION

### 6. TRAINING ROOM — 2:17–2:36

Return to the opening training room. It is now pristine: cleaner negative,
steadier gate, richer color, no scratches.

Mara stands behind the lectern in a celadon instructor’s suit. Her field jacket
is gone. The red binder is open before her. June’s mug sits at her right hand.

There is no transformation shot. This is simply the approved version.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**  
Approved instruction supersedes lived experience.

Mara looks down at a line in the binder. She knows she is expected to read it.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**  
Mara’s objection created a personnel discrepancy. Reassignment was economical.

Mara reads without emphasis:

**MARA**  
If discrepancies remain, update the employee.

A red stamp fills frame:

```text
CONTINUITY RESTORED
```

Cut to a completion certificate:

```text
COMPLETED BY: MARA VOSS

[ ]
```

Mara’s left hand enters; her watch identifies it. She places the pencil beside
the box without checking it.

The Department stamps `CONTINUITY RESTORED` across the empty checkbox anyway.

The three-note chime resolves cleanly.

Cut to black.

---

## Spoken-word count and performance

The dialogue manifest is the source of truth for spoken words and timing. It
must remain at or below 205 words. A measured scratch narration must occupy no
more than 68% of the 156-second film. Silence is preserved
for:

- the receipt-date correction;
- Peter’s second loop and direct question;
- the photograph replacement;
- the transfer of recognition;
- Mara discovering herself in the school photograph;
- the unchecked completion box.

Nobody performs “surreal.” The actors behave as if accuracy, not reality, is at
stake.
